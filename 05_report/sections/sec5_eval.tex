\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

\subsection{Test Environment}
All experiments ran on a MacBook Air M1 (8\,GB RAM) with MySQL Community Server 26.7.0, on the 60{,}000-trip dataset loaded via the repository's \texttt{build.sql} and \texttt{import\_data.py} (seed 534). Timings are single-run values taken from Workbench's Action Output (query execution time, excluding result-set transfer). No warm-up or repetition was applied; figures should be read as indicative magnitudes rather than stable benchmarks.

\subsection{Experiment 1: Index Before/After}
Target query and index creation:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=SQL]
SELECT COUNT(*) FROM Trip
WHERE status = 'completed' AND start_time >= '2026-10-01';
CREATE INDEX idx_trip_status_time ON Trip (status, start_time);
\end{lstlisting}
With no secondary index on those columns the plan is a full table scan (\texttt{type=ALL}, rows $\approx$60{,}261). After the composite index --- equality column first, range column second, leftmost-prefix rule --- the plan switches to a covering index range scan (\texttt{Using index}), no table lookups.

\midrule

\subsection{Experiment 2: JOIN vs Subquery}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=SQL]
-- NOT EXISTS
SELECT COUNT(*) FROM Trip t WHERE t.status = 'completed'
  AND NOT EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM Payment p WHERE p.trip_id = t.trip_id);
-- NOT IN
SELECT COUNT(*) FROM Trip t WHERE t.status = 'completed'
  AND t.trip_id NOT IN (SELECT trip_id FROM Payment);
-- LEFT JOIN ... IS NULL
SELECT COUNT(*) FROM Trip t LEFT JOIN Payment p ON p.trip_id = t.trip_id
  WHERE t.status = 'completed' AND p.pay_id IS NULL;
\end{lstlisting}
All three returned the same 1{,}442 rows and identical plans: full scan of \texttt{Trip} probed against the unique index on \texttt{Payment.trip\_id} (\texttt{eq\_ref}), with \texttt{Extra: Not exists; Using index} --- the optimizer rewrote both subquery forms into the same anti-join.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Three anti-join formulations on the same question.}
\label{tab:exp2}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
Formulation & result & plan signature & actual time \\
\midrule
\texttt{NOT EXISTS}          & 1{,}442 & \texttt{Not exists; Using index} & 89 ms \\
\texttt{NOT IN}              & 1{,}442 & same as above & 82 ms \\
\texttt{LEFT JOIN ... IS NULL} & 1{,}442 & same type, minor \texttt{filtered} diff & 90 ms \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

NOT IN is safe only when the subquery column is NOT NULL (\texttt{Payment.trip\_id} is). Query-level consistency shows the three forms agree; it does not independently verify zero missed trips against a known expected set.

\subsection{Experiment 3: Concurrent Transactions}
Two Workbench windows (separate connections) on the same database, row \texttt{trip\_id=92} of \texttt{trip\_detail}. The interleaving:

\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Step & Session A & Session B \\
\midrule
1 & START TRANSACTION; & \\
  & SELECT rating INTO @a \ldots; -- reads 3 & \\
2 & & START TRANSACTION; \\
  & & SELECT rating INTO @b \ldots; -- reads 3 \\
3 & UPDATE SET rating = @a+1; COMMIT; & \\
4 & & UPDATE SET rating = @b-1; COMMIT; \\
5 & SELECT \ldots; -- reads 2 & \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\vspace{4pt}
\noindent Result: 2 --- A's increment is lost (both UPDATEs reported ``1 row(s) affected''). With \texttt{SELECT \ldots FOR UPDATE} at step 1, B blocks at step 2 until A commits; B then reads the committed 3 (not the stale copy) and both increments survive ($2 \to 3 \to 4$). For the isolation-level comparison on row \texttt{trip\_id=150}: under REPEATABLE READ the transaction keeps seeing its snapshot (3 even after an external commit of 5); under READ COMMITTED it reads the latest committed value each time (5, then 3).

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Concurrency scenarios and constraint interceptions.}
\label{tab:exp3}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Scenario & Observation & What it shows \\
\midrule
Lost update & final rating 2, one write lost & the failure mode \\
\texttt{FOR UPDATE} & B waits, then $2\to3\to4$ & row lock fixes it \\
RR re-read & still 3 after external commit & snapshot read \\
RC re-read & sees 5, then sees 3 & reads latest commit \\
FK to missing trip        & \texttt{ERROR 1452} & referential integrity \\
Duplicate \texttt{trip\_id} payment & \texttt{ERROR 1062} & 1:1 via UNIQUE \\
Rating out of 1--5 range  & \texttt{ERROR 3819} & CHECK constraint \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The last three rows also serve as evidence for G5: the constraints themselves stop illegal writes at the schema layer.

\subsection{Distribution Check against the RideAustin Holdout}
Generator parameters came from an 80/20 split of RideAustin by RIDE\_ID hash (deterministic, no temporal cut). We compared four marginal statistics against the held-out 20\%.

What this checks is that several marginal statistics are close --- it does not validate the full joint distribution, long tails, or business-level external validity. Status mix, payment methods and the unpaid rate follow course-defined rules with no source counterpart.

\subsection{Results Summary and Goal Recheck}
The composite index reduced a full scan to a covering range scan (16.6\,ms $\to$ 0.46\,ms, $\approx$36$\times$) --- a single-run measurement, not a P95 verification. The three anti-join formulations produced identical results and plans; query-form agreement, not independent zero-missed verification. The lost-update experiment showed the read-modify-write hazard and its \texttt{FOR UPDATE} fix; the error codes 1452/1062/3819 confirmed schema-level constraint interception. Together these results partially address the measurable goals of Section~\ref{sec:context}; G2 and G3 remain unverified at the workload level.
