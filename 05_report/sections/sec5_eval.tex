\subsection{Workload Profile}
% 素材：A3 v1.1（Top5 = Q6/Q1/Q5/Q2/Q7）+ A6 读写分流；TODO(A): A5 定稿后校对一行
The workload follows the read/write split argued in Section~\ref{sec:context}: transactional writes (dispatch and payment) demand strong consistency, while analytical reads tolerate weaker freshness. The five most frequent queries are Q6 (hourly dispatch monitoring), Q1 (daily reconciliation), Q5 (periodic collections), Q2 (collections by driver) and Q7 (monthly reporting); all ten queries of Section~\ref{sec:sql} are read-only SELECTs, and writes concentrate on \texttt{Trip} and \texttt{Payment} at dispatch and settlement time.

\subsection{Experiment 1: Index Before/After}
% 载体与判读依据：02_sql/exp_index.sql §1（C 预验值注释）；G2 靶子 P95<200ms
The target query counts completed trips since 2026-10 (\texttt{status} equality + \texttt{start\_time} range). Before the index, the plan is a full table scan (\texttt{type=ALL}, key NULL, rows $\approx$60{,}261); a composite index \texttt{(status, start\_time)} --- leftmost prefix: equality column first, range column second --- changes the plan to an index range scan whose leaves already contain everything the query needs (\texttt{Using index}, covering scan).

The same experiment exposes the classic non-sargable trap: wrapping the column in a function (\texttt{DATE(start\_time) = '2026-10-15'}) degrades access to a full sequential index scan, while rewriting it as a half-open interval restores \texttt{type=range}. This directly justifies the hour-bucket rewrite note of Q6. Against the latency target G2 (P95 $<$ 200 ms), the indexed plan stays two orders of magnitude below the bar.

\subsection{Experiment 2: JOIN vs Subquery}
The anti-join ``completed trips without payment'' (Q2/Q5) is expressed three ways: \texttt{NOT EXISTS}, \texttt{NOT IN}, and \texttt{LEFT JOIN ... IS NULL}. All three return the same 1{,}442 rows, and their plans coincide: a full scan of \texttt{Trip} probe-joined against the unique index of \texttt{Payment.trip\_id} (\texttt{eq\_ref}), with \texttt{Extra: Not exists; Using index} --- the optimizer rewrote both subquery forms into the same anti-join as the explicit LEFT JOIN variant.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Three anti-join formulations: identical semantics, near-identical cost.}
\label{tab:exp2}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
Formulation & result & plan signature & actual time \\
\midrule
\texttt{NOT EXISTS}          & 1{,}442 & \texttt{Not exists; Using index} & 89 ms \\
\texttt{NOT IN}              & 1{,}442 & same as above & 82 ms \\
\texttt{LEFT JOIN ... IS NULL} & 1{,}442 & same type, minor \texttt{filtered} diff & 90 ms \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

One caveat travels with \texttt{NOT IN}: it is only safe when the subquery column is NOT NULL (\texttt{Payment.trip\_id} is), otherwise it silently returns nothing --- a reminder that plan evidence, not habit, should pick the formulation (conceptual Q4).

\subsection{Experiment 3: Concurrent Transactions}
Two Workbench tabs simulate concurrent sessions on \texttt{trip\_detail} (row \texttt{trip\_id=92}, rating 3). \emph{Scenario 1 (lost update):} both transactions read 3 into session variables, A writes $3{+}1{=}4$ and commits, B then writes $3{-}1{=}2$ from its stale copy --- A's update vanishes although both statements reported success. \emph{Scenario 2 (pessimistic lock):} A's \texttt{SELECT ... FOR UPDATE} blocks B at the same statement; after A commits, B reads the committed value (current read) and both increments survive ($2 \to 3 \to 4$). \emph{Scenario 3 (isolation levels):} under the default REPEATABLE READ a transaction re-reads its snapshot (still 3) after another session commits a change, while READ COMMITTED sees the committed 5/3 flips --- snapshot reads versus current reads, exactly the behaviour that makes \texttt{FOR UPDATE} necessary in Scenario 2 (conceptual Q5).

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Concurrency scenarios and constraint interceptions (G5).}
\label{tab:exp3}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Scenario & Observation & Guard demonstrated \\
\midrule
Lost update (read-modify-write) & final rating 2, one update lost & --- (the failure mode) \\
\texttt{SELECT ... FOR UPDATE} & B waits, then $2\to3\to4$ & row lock (isolation layer) \\
RR vs.\ RC re-read & RR keeps snapshot 3; RC sees 5/3 & isolation-level semantics \\

Duplicate \texttt{trip\_id} payment & \texttt{ERROR 1062} & 1:1 via UNIQUE \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The last three rows double as evidence for goal G5: the declared constraints themselves intercept illegal writes at the schema layer, independent of any application logic.

\subsection{Results Summary and Goal Recheck}
(1) The composite index cut estimated rows from 60{,}261 to 3{,}259 and actual latency from 16.6 ms to 0.46 ms ($\approx$36$\times$) --- far inside the 200 ms P95 target G2. (2) Three anti-join formulations agree in result (1{,}442 rows) and plan, so query choice is governed by semantics and EXPLAIN evidence rather than folklore. (3) Concurrency behaviour decomposes cleanly: the lost update is a read-modify-write artefact, \texttt{FOR UPDATE} eliminates it, and schema-level constraints (1452/1062/3819) intercept illegal writes without application help (G5). Together with the holdout check these results close the loop with the measurable goals of Section~\ref{sec:context}.
