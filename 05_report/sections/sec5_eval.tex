\subsection{Test Environment}
All experiments ran on a MacBook Air M1 (8\,GB RAM) with MySQL Community Server 26.7.0, on the 60{,}000-trip dataset loaded via the repository's \texttt{build.sql} and \texttt{import\_data.py} (seed 534). Timings are single-run values taken from Workbench's Action Output (query execution time, excluding result-set transfer). No warm-up or repetition was applied; figures should be read as indicative magnitudes rather than stable benchmarks.

\subsection{Experiment 1: Index Before/After}
The target query counts completed trips accepted since 2026-10-01; the experiment then creates the composite index \texttt{idx\_trip\_status\_time} on \texttt{Trip(status, start\_time)} --- both statements verbatim in Appendix~\ref{app:expsql}, and the before/after screenshot pair in Appendix~\ref{app:screens} (Figure~\ref{fig:exp1}).
With no secondary index on those columns the plan is a full table scan (\texttt{type=ALL}, rows $\approx$60{,}261). After the composite index --- equality column first, range column second, leftmost-prefix rule --- the plan switches to a covering index range scan (\texttt{Using index}), no table lookups.

Wrapping the indexed column in a function (\texttt{DATE(start\_time) = ...}) degrades access to a sequential index scan; rewriting as a half-open interval restores \texttt{type=range}. This single measurement shows the index eliminates the full scan; it does not constitute a measured P95 across the Top-5 workload queries.

\subsection{Experiment 2: JOIN vs Subquery}
One question --- count completed trips without a payment row --- in three formulations: \texttt{NOT EXISTS}, \texttt{NOT IN} and \texttt{LEFT JOIN \ldots IS NULL} (statements verbatim in Appendix~\ref{app:expsql}).
Each returned a single result row with the same count, 1{,}442, under identical plans: a full scan of \texttt{Trip} probed against the unique index on \texttt{Payment.trip\_id} (\texttt{eq\_ref}), with \texttt{Extra: Not exists; Using index} --- the optimizer rewrote both subquery forms into the same anti-join.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Three anti-join formulations on the same question.}
\label{tab:exp2}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
Formulation & result & plan signature & actual time \\
\midrule
\texttt{NOT EXISTS}          & 1{,}442 & \texttt{Not exists; Using index} & 89 ms \\
\texttt{NOT IN}              & 1{,}442 & same as above & 82 ms \\
\texttt{LEFT JOIN ... IS NULL} & 1{,}442 & same type, minor \texttt{filtered} diff & 90 ms \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

NOT IN is safe only when the subquery column is NOT NULL (\texttt{Payment.trip\_id} is). Query-level consistency shows the three forms agree; it does not independently verify zero missed trips against a known expected set.

\subsection{Experiment 3: Concurrent Transactions}
Two Workbench windows (separate connections) on the same database, row \texttt{trip\_id=92} of \texttt{trip\_detail}. The interleaving:

% 2026-11-12 E：Times 下此表超宽 7.8pt，\small 收回
{\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Step & Session A & Session B \\
\midrule
1 & START TRANSACTION; & \\
  & SELECT rating INTO @a \ldots; -- reads 3 & \\
2 & & START TRANSACTION; \\
  & & SELECT rating INTO @b \ldots; -- reads 3 \\
3 & UPDATE SET rating = @a+1; COMMIT; & \\
4 & & UPDATE SET rating = @b-1; COMMIT; \\
5 & SELECT \ldots; -- reads 2 & \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
}\vspace{4pt}
\noindent Scenario 1 (lost update) ends at 2: A's increment is overwritten even though both UPDATEs reported ``1 row(s) affected''. Scenario 2 then reruns the demonstration with locking, on the same row and its leftover value of 2, this time with both sessions incrementing: A opens with \texttt{SELECT \ldots FOR UPDATE}, so B's locking read blocks until A commits (\texttt{rating = rating + 1} writes $2\to3$); released, B's locking read is a current read --- it sees the committed 3, not the stale snapshot --- and writes $3\to4$. Both increments survive, and nothing was reset by hand. A third, independent comparison on row \texttt{trip\_id=150} (initial value 3) contrasts isolation levels: under REPEATABLE READ the transaction keeps seeing its snapshot (still 3 after another session commits 5); under READ COMMITTED each read returns the latest committed value (5, then 3).

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Concurrency scenarios and constraint interceptions.}
\label{tab:exp3}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Scenario & Observation & What it shows \\
\midrule
Lost update & final rating 2, one write lost & the failure mode \\
\texttt{FOR UPDATE} & B waits, then $2\to3\to4$ & row lock fixes it \\
RR re-read & still 3 after external commit & snapshot read \\
RC re-read & sees 5, then sees 3 & reads latest commit \\
FK to missing trip        & \texttt{ERROR 1452} & referential integrity \\

The last three rows also serve as evidence for G5: the constraints themselves stop illegal writes at the schema layer.

\subsection{Descriptive Distribution Check against RideAustin}
The generator's parameters were calibrated from full-dataset RideAustin statistics (Section~\ref{subsec:dataset}); no train/holdout split was used. Table~\ref{tab:holdout} is therefore a descriptive check that the generated sample reproduces the marginals it was calibrated against --- not an independent validation on held-out data. Methods: the RideAustin column uses all 1{,}494{,}125 rides (tier shares from \texttt{requested\_car\_category}; mean \texttt{total\_fare}; \texttt{driver\_rating} non-NULL share; 17--20h share computed from \texttt{created\_date} in US Central time), and the generated column uses the 60{,}000-trip sample (mean \texttt{Trip.fare} over the 49{,}200 completed trips; rating coverage over completed trips; 17--20h share over all trips by \texttt{start\_time}, the dispatch-acceptance timestamp). Results and field notes: \texttt{01\_docs/d5\_holdout\_comparison.md} in the repository.

All four dimensions agree within one percentage point, and the mean fare within one cent. The check covers marginal statistics only --- not the joint distribution, long tails, or business-level external validity. Status mix, payment methods and the unpaid rate follow course-defined rules with no source counterpart.
