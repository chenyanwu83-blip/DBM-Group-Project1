\section{Business Context and Goals}
\label{sec:context}

\subsection{Background and Business Process}
\label{subsec:background}

We model the order-to-fulfilment chain of a ride-hailing platform: a passenger requests a ride, the platform dispatches it to a driver, the trip is performed and paid for, and optionally rated. Five official entities carry this chain---\texttt{Passenger}, \texttt{Driver}, \texttt{Vehicle}, \texttt{Trip} and \texttt{Payment}---joined by two non-default cardinalities: every driver is bound to exactly one vehicle (\texttt{Driver.vehicle\_id} UNIQUE, $N{:}1$), and every trip carries at most one payment (\texttt{Payment.trip\_id} UNIQUE, $1{:}1$).

One scoping decision shapes all downstream analysis (decision D-05): request--driver matching happens in the application layer, and a \texttt{Trip} row is inserted only when dispatch succeeds (\texttt{driver\_id} is \texttt{NOT NULL} in the official DDL). The database therefore never observes unmatched requests, and order-volume or dispatch-latency analytics are out of scope; we acknowledge this in Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}. Once inserted, a trip moves \texttt{accepted} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{en\_route} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{completed}, or is cancelled with two billed outcomes: cancelling within the grace period is free, while late cancellation is charged a flat \$1.50 fee and still produces exactly one \texttt{Payment} row---any charge, including a cancellation fee, must be a durable transactional record (decision D-06; first-party cancellation policy~\cite{ubercancelfee}, transactional-record practice~\cite{uberengmysql}).

\label{subsec:goals}

\textbf{Consistency trade-off.} Consistency is tiered by access path rather than chosen globally (A6). The dispatch/payment \emph{write path} is strongly consistent: money and contractual obligations behave like bank transfers, so invalid writes must be rejected by the database itself---transactions plus FK/UNIQUE/CHECK constraints, ``reject rather than silently accept''---and the 1:1 UNIQUE key on \texttt{Payment.trip\_id} is itself the consistency promise that makes anti-join reconciliation (G4) semantically sound. Aggregate \emph{reads} (reconciliation reports, monthly GMV, the 5-minute dispatch dashboard) may be weakly consistent: they consume trends and summaries, where minute-level staleness does not change decisions. Our workload profile justifies the split: by statement count the load is write-dominated ($\approx$99:1), yet by rows scanned it is read-dominated ($>$95\%), so routing wide aggregations to a read replica or cache leaves the primary for single-row writes and keeps its index maintenance affordable. Point lookups stay strongly consistent: customer-service lookups must see the latest trip status. In this course project we run a single instance, so the replica tier is a declared design stance rather than a deployed component (Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}).

\subsection{Dataset Selection Rationale}
\label{subsec:dataset}

The dataset is synthetic: \texttt{import\_data.py} generates it with a fixed seed, so every member rebuilds identical data from the same repository scripts (constraints C6 and D-08). Its distributional parameters are calibrated against RideAustin~\cite{rideaustin-data,zuniga2020rideaustin}---a real, open, CC0-licensed ride-hailing corpus of $\approx$1.49M trips (Austin, 2016--2017)---covering the four-tier vehicle mix (REGULAR 95.1\%, SUV 3.7\%, PREMIUM 0.9\%, LUXURY 0.3\%), per-trip ratings ($\approx$90\% coverage on completed trips), four-tier fare rates, and the $\approx$20\% evening-peak share (17:00--20:00, realised as 20.5\% in the sample).

Real datasets were rejected on \emph{field fit}, a decisive criterion for a project graded on relationship modelling: NYC TLC high-volume FHV records~\cite{tlctripdata} carry no passenger identity, no payment method, and no driver--vehicle 1:1 binding---precisely the relationship columns this schema exists to enforce. To our knowledge, RideAustin is the only public dataset offering per-trip ratings, service tier, and both party IDs simultaneously, which is why it serves as the calibration source rather than the corpus itself. The sample scale is 60{,}000 trips over 365 days (satisfying G1 with margin), of which 1{,}442 \texttt{completed} trips deliberately lack payments so that the reconciliation anti-join is non-empty and verifiable (G4). Two honest caveats: the pickup-zone distribution (15 zones, top-3 $\approx$35\%) is a course setting without a calibration source and is declared as such; and an 80/20 calibration/holdout split of RideAustin underpins the distributional comparison reported in Section~\ref{sec:eval} (decision D-12).
