\section{Business Context and Goals}
\label{sec:context}

\subsection{Background and Business Process}
\label{subsec:background}

This project models a ride-hailing platform from dispatch to trip completion, payment and optional rating. The official schema contains five base entities, \texttt{Passenger}, \texttt{Driver}, \texttt{Vehicle}, \texttt{Trip} and \texttt{Payment}. The project retains these five tables and adds \texttt{trip\_detail}, \texttt{service\_tier} and \texttt{pickup\_zone}, giving eight entities in total. The foreign key, \texttt{NOT NULL} and \texttt{UNIQUE} constraints on \texttt{Driver.vehicle\_id} assign each driver one vehicle and each vehicle at most one driver. Similarly, \texttt{Payment.trip\_id} is a unique foreign key, so each trip can have at most one payment.

\texttt{trip\_detail} links each trip to its service tier and pickup zone and stores an optional rating. Its primary key, \texttt{trip\_id}, also serves as a foreign key to \texttt{Trip}. Two further foreign keys reference \texttt{service\_tier} and \texttt{pickup\_zone}. The generator creates one detail record per trip, including cancelled trips. The primary key and foreign key constrain each trip to at most one detail record.

Under decision D-05, request matching takes place in the application layer. A \texttt{Trip} row is inserted only after successful dispatch, with \texttt{start\_time} recording that time. Unmatched requests are therefore unavailable for analysing total ride demand or matching latency (Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}). Trip status progresses through \texttt{accepted}, \texttt{en\_route} and \texttt{completed}. Early termination after boarding is classified as \texttt{completed}. Under D-06, cancellation before boarding is free within the grace period. Late cancellation or a passenger no-show incurs a \$1.50 fee, subject to a waiver for excessive pickup delays. Each charge, including a cancellation fee, is recorded in \texttt{Payment}~\cite{ubercancelfee,uberengmysql}.

\subsection{Quantified Goals, Constraints and Trade-offs}
\label{subsec:goals}

Table~\ref{tab:goals} distinguishes project design targets from the available verification evidence. The 200\,ms latency target, provisional 50 TPS throughput target and 180-day retention period are project requirements. Constraints include 180-day online retention with archiving (C1), relationship cardinalities enforced through UNIQUE constraints (C2), non-negative payment amounts enforced through CHECK (C3), compatibility with the group's lowest MySQL version, 8.0.45 (C5), and rebuilding within 30 minutes using repository scripts and a fixed random seed (C6).

\textbf{Workload assumption for G3.} Assuming 50{,}000 trips per day, with 20\% occurring within two to four hours, the estimated arrival rate is 0.69--1.39 trips per second. Converting this rate to database TPS requires defining the transactions and their boundaries for each trip. The 50 TPS target remains unvalidated.

\textbf{Consistency trade-off.} Dispatch and payment writes require strong consistency, while customer-service lookups require the latest trip status. Transactions and FK, UNIQUE and CHECK constraints protect data integrity. Aggregate reads, including reconciliation reports, monthly revenue summaries and the five-minute dispatch dashboard, can tolerate minute-level staleness. The A5 workload model estimates that writes account for approximately 99\% of statements, while aggregate reads account for over 95\% of scanned rows. These estimates motivate routing aggregate queries to a read replica or cache. The project runs on a single MySQL instance, so replication remains a design proposal (Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}).

\subsection{Dataset Selection Rationale}
\label{subsec:dataset}

\texttt{import\_data.py} generates 60{,}000 trips using a fixed random seed, allowing all members to rebuild the same dataset (C6, D-08). RideAustin~\cite{rideaustin-data,zuniga2020rideaustin}, a CC0-licensed dataset of approximately 1.49 million trips in Austin during 2016--2017, provides calibration data for service tiers, fares, rating coverage and the evening peak (17:00--20:00). An 80/20 calibration/holdout split supports the comparison in Section~\ref{sec:eval} (D-12). The generated REGULAR/SUV/PREMIUM/LUXURY shares are 95.1/3.7/0.9/0.3\%, with approximately 90\% rating coverage on completed trips. Section~\ref{sec:eval} reports an evening-peak share of 22.4\% in the generated data and approximately 20.5\% in the holdout.

Field compatibility motivates using synthetic data. NYC TLC high-volume FHV records~\cite{tlctripdata} lack the passenger identifiers, payment methods and driver--vehicle assignments required by the schema. RideAustin provides ratings, service tiers and passenger/driver identifiers, making it suitable for calibration. The sample spans 365 days for query demonstrations, while 180-day online retention remains a design constraint. It intentionally includes 1{,}442 completed trips without payment for reconciliation tests. Cancellation rules, payment methods, the unpaid proportion and 15 pickup zones (top three approximately 35\%) are course assumptions. These dimensions are excluded from the RideAustin validation.
