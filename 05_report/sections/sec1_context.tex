\section{Business Context and Goals}
\label{sec:context}

\subsection{Business Context}
\label{subsec:background}

This project develops a relational database for a ride-hailing platform to support trip queries, payment reconciliation and operational reporting. It retains five base entities, \texttt{Passenger}, \texttt{Driver}, \texttt{Vehicle}, \texttt{Trip} and \texttt{Payment}, and adds \texttt{trip\_detail}, \texttt{service\_tier} and \texttt{pickup\_zone}. These eight entities connect trips with their participants, payments, service tiers, pickup zones and optional ratings.

Foreign keys and unique constraints maintain valid relationships between records. Each driver is assigned one vehicle, each vehicle has at most one driver, and each trip has at most one payment. The generator also creates one detail record for every trip.

The recorded workflow begins after successful dispatch, when the driver accepts the request. Unmatched requests therefore fall outside the database scope. Trip status progresses through \texttt{accepted}, \texttt{en\_route} and \texttt{completed}. Early termination after boarding is classified as completed. Before boarding, cancellation may be free or charged. The course-defined cancellation fee is USD~1.50, subject to a waiver for excessive pickup delays. Actual charges are recorded in \texttt{Payment}~\cite{ubercancelfee,uberengmysql}.

\subsection{Stakeholder Requirements}
\label{subsec:stakeholders}

Passengers need trip histories and fare/payment reconciliation. Drivers need cumulative trip and fare totals and verifiable vehicle assignments. Operations staff require peak-hour trip volumes, cancellation rates and assignment information. Finance staff need unpaid-trip reconciliation and payment totals by method. Customer service requires complete trip records within 30 seconds to investigate disputed fares.

These requirements guide the relationships, integrity constraints and queries developed in the following sections.

\subsection{Goals and Constraints}
\label{subsec:goals}

The project defines five measurable goals. G1 requires at least 50{,}000 trips. G2 sets a P95 execution-time target below 200\,ms for the five high-frequency queries, Q6, Q1, Q5, Q2 and Q7. G3 proposes a provisional write-throughput target of approximately 50 transactions per second. G4 requires all completed but unpaid trips to be identified within 500\,ms. G5 requires zero orphan records and database-level rejection of writes that violate constraints.

These goals require different verification methods. Row-count checks establish sample size, while repeated execution of complete queries is needed to measure P95. Throughput testing requires a defined transaction workload, and reconciliation completeness requires an independently established set of expected trip identifiers. Constraint tests examine whether the database rejects invalid writes. The available evidence and outstanding tests are discussed in Sections~\ref{sec:eval} and~\ref{sec:conclusion}.

Dispatch and payment operations require consistent data, while customer-service queries require the latest trip status. Periodic reports can tolerate brief delays, making a read replica a possible future extension. The current implementation uses one MySQL instance. Other constraints include compatibility with MySQL 8.0.45, rebuilding within 30 minutes using fixed-seed repository scripts, and a proposed 180-day online retention policy with archival of older records.

\subsection{Dataset Selection}
\label{subsec:dataset}

The project uses synthetic data because the public datasets considered do not provide all the identifiers, vehicle assignments and payment relationships required by the schema~\cite{tlctripdata}. Fixed-seed generation allows members to rebuild the same dataset and reproduce query results.

Generation parameters reference full-dataset RideAustin statistics for service tiers, fares and ratings~\cite{rideaustin-data,zuniga2020rideaustin}. Comparisons with these statistics describe similarities in selected measures. Independent holdout validation requires confirmation that parameter estimation and evaluation use separate records. Cancellation rules, payment methods, unpaid rates and the 15 pickup zones are course-defined assumptions.

The final sample contains 60{,}000 trips spanning one year, supporting monthly reporting demonstrations. It intentionally includes 1{,}442 completed trips without payment records for reconciliation tests. The sample includes historical records, while the 180-day online retention and archival policy remains a design requirement.
