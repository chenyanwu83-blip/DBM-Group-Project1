\subsection{Limitations}
Four boundaries should be read alongside these results. First, the dataset is synthetic: the distributional agreement with the RideAustin holdout (Section~\ref{sec:eval}) reflects the generator's calibration rules rather than real-world fit, so we report similarity and make no causal claims; the 15-zone pickup distribution in particular is a declared course setting without a calibration source. Second, by decision D-05 a trip enters the database only when dispatch succeeds, so unmatched requests are structurally invisible: order volume, dispatch success rate and matching latency cannot be analysed in this schema. Third, all performance figures are single-run magnitudes on one laptop-scale MySQL instance at 60{,}000 rows; they are order-of-magnitude evidence, not measured percentiles, and do not extrapolate to production scale or hardware---on this single instance the read-replica tier of the consistency design (Section~\ref{subsec:goals}) remains a declared stance rather than a deployed component. Fourth, the sample is thin in its tails: with 150 drivers and $\approx$180 LUXURY trips, per-tier and per-driver aggregates are illustrative rather than statistically significant.

\subsection{Future Work}
Three extensions follow naturally. Partitioning \texttt{Trip} by \texttt{start\_time} would turn the 180-day retention window into a drop-partition archival job instead of row-by-row deletes, and pairs well with the composite index of Experiment~1---at a real cost: MySQL 8.0 allows no foreign keys on partitioned InnoDB tables, and \texttt{Trip} sits on both sides (its own FKs, plus \texttt{Payment} and \texttt{trip\_detail} referencing it), so the gain would be paid for in declarative referential integrity, re-established in the application layer. Deploying the declared read replica would route the wide aggregate reads (reconciliation reports, monthly revenue, the dispatch dashboard) away from the write-heavy primary. Finally, a pre-insert matching-event table (request, driver offer, dispatch outcome) would close the D-05 scope gap and bring dispatch analytics within reach of the same five-entity core; re-modelling that event stream against a document store (NoSQL comparison) would be the natural next experiment.
